## 1. What she asked for

- **Problems with the current site:** doesn't bring in enough customers; hard to use on phones.
- **Style:** clean and minimal, plus fun and colorful.
- **Features:** automated emails and follow-ups, custom integrations (CRM etc.), Enterprise features. This is the future full-site scope, not the homepage mockup.
- **Assets:** she has her logo, photos and reviews ready.
- **Next step if she loves it:** talk it through on a call first.
- **Timeline:** homepage as soon as possible; full redesign (homepage plus a sales page for each product) live by the start of 2027.
- **Brand colors to keep:** #000000, #ffffff, #38e58b (bright green), #09761d (darker green "for balance").

### Homepage priorities (her words)
She has many offers (digital products, community, consultations, services, programs), so the homepage easily becomes decision overload. **Feature only three offers:**
1. **Development Notes** (signature service)
2. **Workbooks** (Idea to Script Starter Kits)
3. **Community** (where courses will also live)

Everything else gets its own landing page and lives in the footer site map.

### Traffic and checkout facts that shape the design
- Most visitors reach her **sales pages directly** from third-party links.
- The **homepage** is mostly reached through **Google searches for her name or the company**. Those visitors already know of her, so the homepage should confirm who she is fast, build trust and route people to one of three offers.
- Most purchases go through **Stripe payment links**, and most also require a **form as part of checkout**. Buttons can link to Stripe or to the form step; no cart needs building.

## 2. What she likes about the reference (showrunher.com/next)

The reference is a single-offer sales page, not a homepage. She likes it for:
- **A clear offer:** visitors instantly know what it is and whether it's for them.
- **Answering objections:** a "Finishing the draft opens a whole new set of questions" block, a "How this could help you" card grid by situation, "Who should attend," and FAQs.
- **Large typography:** a condensed display font in all caps (Big Shoulders Display), Public Sans for body text, and IBM Plex Mono for small labels.
- **Visual flow and story journey:** problem, then what we cover, then "is this you?", then who it's for and what you leave with, then the instructor, then the offer and price, then FAQ, then a final call to action.
- **Section composition:** varied layouts that alternate dark and cream sections, with a single bright accent colour (yellow) for calls to action. Every section has one clear job.

Her complaint about her own site: **"My current site has a redundant layout."**

Takeaways to borrow (not copy):
- One idea per section, a big headline and short supporting copy.
- Speak to the visitor's situation ("You finished your script…") before selling.
- Small mono labels ("THE RUN OF SHOW") as section eyebrows.
- A strong dark/light rhythm with one accent colour. For her, that's the green.
- A clear "who it's for / not for" and an FAQ to answer objections.

## 4. Brand colours: accessibility notes

| Combo | Contrast | OK for |
|---|---|---|
| Black on #38e58b | 12.75:1 | ✅ all text (buttons, badges) |
| White on #38e58b | 1.65:1 | ❌ never for text |
| #38e58b on black | 12.75:1 | ✅ accent text and headlines on dark sections |
| #38e58b on white | 1.65:1 | ❌ not for text on light sections; decoration only |
| #09761d on white | 5.8:1 | ✅ accent text and links on light sections |
| White on #09761d | 5.8:1 | ✅ buttons and bands |
| #09761d on black | 3.62:1 | ⚠️ large text only |

Rule of thumb: **bright green on dark sections, dark green on light sections.** Green buttons use black text.

## 5. MigsFlow standards that apply (Handbook v1.2 + past projects)

- **Structure:** root `index.html`; `css/` (global.css, styles.css), `javascript/` (global.js, script.js), `images/`, `fonts/`, other pages in `html/`.
  - ⚠️ The folder currently has root-level `styles.css` and `main.js` (empty). Move them to `css/styles.css` and `javascript/script.js` to match the handbook and the Undrxposur and Velaisance redesigns.
- **Separation of concerns:** no inline CSS or JS.
- **Mobile-first:** use `min-width` media queries, and test between breakpoints.
- **Fonts:** self-hosted in `fonts/`, `font-display: swap`, only the weights used. No Google Fonts in production.
- **Images:**
  - compressed WebP plus a JPG fallback, with `-m` crops for mobile, inside `<picture>`
  - `width`/`height` set, `decoding="async"`, and lazy loading below the fold only
- **Icons:** SVG, no Font Awesome or jQuery.
- **Buttons:** colour fill sweeps left to right on hover and focus (300–600 ms), and reduced motion is respected.
- **Nav:** fully responsive, `aria-expanded` on the menu toggle, and mobile sub-menus collapsed until tapped.
- **Accessibility (from Moe's Movers and MigsFlow):**
  - 0 WAVE errors and alerts, and axe clean on desktop and phone
  - icon-only links use visually-hidden text
  - alt text under ~70 characters
  - no translucent backgrounds behind text (WAVE reads them as solid)
  - no "possible heading" bold paragraphs
  - no duplicate side-by-side links
  - fade-ins use `filter: opacity()` rather than `opacity`
- **Smooth scroll:** Lenis, hosted locally in `javascript/vendor` (as on Undrxposur).
- **Deliverables (as for Undrxposur and Velaisance):** a snapshot or PDF preview for the client in `documentation/Client Deliverables`.
- **Tone:** friendly and encouraging, not fear-based.

## 6. Early direction (to refine section by section)

The homepage's job is to **confirm who she is, prove results, then send each visitor to one of three doors.**

Possible flow, borrowing the reference's story journey:
1. **Hero:** a big all-caps statement in her voice, one sentence on who TPP is for, a primary button and a secondary one ("Find your starting point").
2. **"Where are you right now?"** The visitor picks their situation, which maps to an offer:
   - "I have an idea but no plan" → Workbooks
   - "I finished a draft" → Development Notes
   - "I want to grow with other writers" → Community
3. **Proof strip:** the stats, plus logos or credits (Disney Writing Program, Syfy, Amazon, FOX).
4. **The three offers,** one clear block each with a price or a "from" price and a single button to its sales page or Stripe link. No 3-format pickers on the homepage; those belong on the sales pages.
5. **Success stories:** Dallas R. and Craig R., short pull quotes with "read more" links.
6. **About Shannan:** a short founder story ("former TV exec… giving you the passcode") with a link to the full About and coaches page.
7. **Who it's for / not for:** short, honest and in her voice.
8. **FAQ:** answers common objections (turnaround, pay later, formats, shorts → Story Jam).
9. **Final CTA:** the free 15-minute chat for anyone still unsure.
10. **Footer site map:** everything else (Story Jams, Academy, competition, Executive Training, blog, coaches, policies).
